# The Explain Loop

> **Canonical master:** [`00-master.md`](00-master.md). Sizing, cut order, and operating rules live there — if this doc and the master disagree, the master wins.

The other three legs of the loop have instructions. This is the leg that collapses into "yeah, I get it" — so it gets a mechanism instead of good intentions.

**Design principles** (so you can trust it, and adapt it when it stops working):

- **Retrieval beats review.** Producing an answer from nothing builds memory. Re-reading builds familiarity, which feels identical and isn't.
- **Speech beats writing.** Written answers let you hedge, edit, and look things up mid-sentence. Speech doesn't. Interviews are spoken, so practice in the medium you'll be tested in.
- **Spacing beats volume.** One question re-asked at 1 / 2 / 4 weeks beats the same question answered once, thoroughly.
- **Probing beats self-grading.** You can't grade your own gaps — you don't know what you don't know. The follow-up question is the instrument.
- **Cheap beats thorough.** 20 min/week survives a busy month. A 3-hour monthly review does not.

## 1. Weekly cold recall (~20 min)

Run in a **fresh chat** with this doc + the current phase doc attached. Prompt:

> Cold recall. Follow `03-explain-loop.md`. Draw 3 questions: 2 from the current phase, 1 from an earlier phase chosen by the re-ask schedule in my log. Ask one at a time, wait for my spoken answer, then probe once with the "why not X / what's the boundary / what breaks it" question. Then color it and tell me exactly what I missed.

**Question sources, in priority order:**
1. The phase's 🎯 drill block.
2. The phase's row in the transfer table ([`00-master.md`](00-master.md)).
3. Any claim you've shipped — provenance, mechanism, boundary (Appendix K in [`91-interview-territory.md`](91-interview-territory.md)).
4. Every Amber and Red in your own log (always eligible).

**The run:**
1. Answer **out loud, no notes, ~2–3 min each.** If you can't start within 15 seconds, that's a Red — don't grind.
2. Take **one probe** per answer. The answer you can't give is the real gap.
3. **Color it:**
   - **Green** — clean, with both mechanism and boundary.
   - **Amber** — right shape, missing mechanism or boundary.
   - **Red** — couldn't produce it.
4. **Log it** in `drills/recall-log.md`: date, phase, question, color, and the exact thing you missed.
5. **Promote:**
   - Amber/Red → an Anki card. Front = the question; back = *your own* answer, transcribed and corrected. Your gaps build the deck, not a premade one.
   - Green → paste the answer into `drills/answers/<topic>.md`. You'll reuse it verbatim in an interview.

**Re-ask schedule (a Leitner box, no app needed):** Red → next week. Amber → 2 weeks. Green → 4 weeks. Green twice → 8 weeks, then retire. The color column in the log *is* the scheduler.

## 2. Monthly recorded walkthrough (~30 min)

Once a month, record **5 minutes, no notes, no edits**: explain the current artifact to an imaginary interviewer — what it does, why this design over the obvious alternative, what you measured, where it breaks.

One take. No re-recording. Watch it back once, and timestamp the moment you started hedging or hand-waving. That timestamp is next month's syllabus.

Keep every recording. The month-1 → month-5 delta is progress evidence, and the last one per artifact is a real interview prop — a walkthrough you can hand someone.

## 3. End-of-phase transfer checkpoint

The five prompts in [`00-master.md`](00-master.md), with one change: **answer them out loud first, then write.** Writing first lets prose hide exactly the gaps that speech exposes.

## The prediction ledger (attached to every measurement)

Before each experiment, write down: the predicted direction of the primary metric, and the predicted direction of the mechanism counter. Run it. Record hit or miss.

A mental model is only real if it predicts. This is the cheapest calibration instrument in the plan, and it turns `perf stat` from a reporting tool into a testing tool. The [`perf stat` Playbook](30-perf-playbook.md) already asks for this in Layer 2 — the ledger makes it a habit rather than an exercise.

Lives at the top of each `bench/results/<experiment>.md`, above the raw output.

## What you have by interview season

| Artifact | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `drills/recall-log.md` | A dated weak-spot list sorted by color. Drill the Reds. |
| `drills/answers/` | Your own answers, already in interview register, already correct |
| `drills/recordings/` | The delta, plus one clean walkthrough per artifact |
| Anki deck | The atomic facts your answers depend on — generated by your own gaps |

## Budget

~20 min/week + ~30 min/month ≈ **1.5 hr/month.** If it costs more than that, it's being done wrong.
